# 01 — Product

## One line

A personal-brand brain for LinkedIn: answer a deep dive about yourself by voice, get a map of everything you can post about, and draft against it so you never run dry and never repeat yourself.

## Who it's for

- Founders, consultants, coaches, and professionals who know they should post on LinkedIn but don't know what to say, or say the same three things.
- People who've tried AI post generators and hated how it sounded.
- Later: small teams where several people post under one business.

Not for: agencies, social media managers running dozens of accounts, anyone wanting mass-produced content.

## Why it works (the thesis)

Most people's LinkedIn content fails for one reason: they post bottom-of-funnel offers to an audience they never built, in a voice that isn't theirs. The fix isn't a better generator. It's knowing yourself well enough to have something real to say, then having a system that keeps you drawing from all of it.

The deep dive produces that self-knowledge. The brain stores it. The studio keeps you honest.

## The modules

### 1. Deep dive (onboarding)

Twelve questions in three sittings, one sitting a day by default (drip, never dump: twenty questions in one go gives twenty thin answers). Keen people can use "or carry on now" and finish in one evening; most finish in three days.

| Sitting | Questions | What it collects | Regions it feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your story | 5 | The scenes: who the work is for, the founding moment, the luck, the leaving, the hardest moment | Whys, Stories |
| What you think | 4 | Beliefs about their industry, earned through experience | Opinions, Whys |
| You, and proof | 3 | Where they're from and who's at home, then what people say and the numbers | Personality, Receipts |

Each question gets its own screen: voice note is the big button, type instead is the small link. Progress is plain text ("Sitting 2 of 3 · question 1 of 4").

After the first answer, three things can happen on the same screen:
- **Second beat.** Most questions carry one fixed follow-up prompt ("And what did you actually say?"). It appears below the first answer with its own record/type control, and the person can skip it. Never more than one per question.
- **Thin-answer nudge.** A voice note under 40 seconds or a typed answer under 60 words gets one line: "That's a start. Say a bit more: what did it actually look like?" Once, and it never blocks moving on.
- **We'd keep this.** Up to three sentences from the answer that contain a name, a place, a number, or quoted speech, shown back to them. Rules only, no AI.

The hardest-moment question carries a safety line, always shown: "Nothing from this goes anywhere until you've decided it should."

Sitting 3 ends with two extra screens:
- **Privacy pass.** Every name mentioned across the three sittings gets an explicit in/out toggle, default out.
- **Screenshots.** A prompt to upload the kind messages, which seeds the photo bank.

**The lock.** The studio stays locked until all three sittings are done and the walkthrough is approved. One exception: after sitting 2, when stories and opinions both exist, the person can write a single draft, labelled "From half a brain". Never more than one until the dive is done.

**Top ups.** The questions that didn't make the twelve go into a queue. After the walkthrough is approved, the brain home offers one a week ("One more for your brain") with the same record/type/second-beat control. Answering it adds a card to its region and tints the lobe; skipping pushes it back a week. Never more than one at a time, and never during the deep dive.

### 2. The brain

The home screen. A side-view brain with seven regions. Click a region to see its cards. Each region's colour fades toward grey the longer it goes unused; the most-neglected lane pulses gently.

After the deep dive, AI extracts cards from the transcripts (in the person's words, marked `constructed` where it compressed). The person approves, edits, or deletes each card. This is the **board walkthrough** moment: they see their whole self laid out for the first time.

Then the app proposes:
- **Engine** — diagnosed from *how* they answered (scenes = Storyteller, frameworks = Teacher, judgements = Commentator, narrating work in progress = Documenter). Shown with the evidence. Person confirms or overrides.
- **Mix** — top/middle/bottom percentages, chosen from their goal (growing an audience / established and selling / full with clients / launching something).
- **Cadence** — posts per week.
- **Headline** — written last, from everything on the board: who they help, what problem they solve, who they are. Three options, they pick or edit.

### 3. Post studio

Draft on the left. On the right, a mini brain lights up as you type, showing which lanes the draft touches. Beneath it:
- the lanes this post is drawing from (detected, editable)
- which funnel level it reads as
- a "gone quiet" list: lanes you haven't posted from in a while, with the card's opening line as a prompt
- a voice check: flags lines that don't sound like their cards (phrasing they've never used, generic LinkedIn-isms)

AI help inside the studio is limited to: tighten this, give me an opening line from this card, suggest three angles on this story. All from approved cards only. Every suggested line is marked constructed until they edit or accept it.

Posts can be saved as drafts, marked as posted (with date), and filed under lanes. Posted posts feed the heat calculation.

### 4. Brain dumps

A loose inbox. Voice or text. "Had a conversation today that…". Not yet cards. Later, the person (or AI, on request) can promote a dump to a card in a region, or straight to a draft.

### 5. Photo bank

Upload personal/branded images. Tag them to regions or cards. The studio surfaces relevant photos when a lane is detected.

### 6. Calendar

Events, launches, talking points. "I'm speaking at X on the 14th." The studio nudges: "You have an event in 9 days and no post about it."

### 7. Feed intelligence (LinkedIn news)

Tips, algorithm updates, trends. Phase 1: the founder posts them (this is the Front of Feed newsletter content, repurposed). Phase 3: members can comment and share their own, which is the forum seed.

### 8. Analytics

Manual at first: the person uploads their LinkedIn export, the app charts impressions, engagement, follower growth against their posting record, and lane usage over time. Exportable as a one-page report (useful when someone has to justify the subscription to a board).

### 9. Community (later)

Comments on feed intelligence posts. Shared tips. Possibly "show your brain" — a public, trimmed view of someone's regions as a profile. Not before phase 3.

## The user journey (first fortnight)

Day 0 — sign up, pick a goal, do the first sitting: your story.
Days 1–2 — one sitting a day, by notification. Or carry on and finish all three in one evening.
Day 2 — after sitting 2, one draft is allowed "from half a brain".
Day 3 — the brain is assembled. Privacy pass. Walkthrough screen. Approve cards. Confirm engine, mix, cadence. Pick a headline.
Day 4 onwards — studio unlocks. First draft, from the lane the app suggests. Mark as posted. Heat starts tracking.
Day 10 — first top up: one more question for your brain. Then one a week.
Day 14 — first weekly digest: what you posted, what's gone quiet, one card you haven't used yet.

## What makes it defensible

- The question set and the follow-up logic are the product. Generators have none of it.
- The commitment: once someone has recorded a dozen voice notes about their life, and keeps adding one a week, they're not switching.
- The brain compounds. Brain dumps, receipts, and posted history make it more useful every month.
- The community later gives it a reason to open daily, not just when writing.

## Pricing (placeholder, decide later)

- Solo: monthly, all modules, one brain.
- Guided: solo + a monthly review with the founder (the current "done with you" offer, productised).
- Team: multiple brains under one business, combined analytics.
